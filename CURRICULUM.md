# Curriculum and problem bank

The curriculum uses selected exercises and teaching methods from the supplied PDFs. New numerical variants and spy-themed contexts are generated locally; worksheet scans and complete answer keys are not embedded in the game.

The sequence groups related topics and builds prerequisites. A new topic starts with its own introductory skill even when the game action has become harder. “Build confidence,” “Stretch” and “Advanced” describe the authored tiers; they are not measured grade levels or claims about test readiness.

This release has 98 generators (17 original, 15 for Shadow Atlas objectives, 6 for vehicle engineering, 24 for Phantom Circuit, 18 for Eclipse Pact, and 18 for Jade Current). Each supports the common three-tier interface; not every generator changes its mathematical structure at every tier. Levels select an explicit generator and tier rather than a random topic-wide question. Replay produces new numbers. The same seed regenerates the same question and answer order when resuming a save.

Fraction guides accept equivalent fractions and decimals. For mixed-number conversions, the checked steps separately ask for the whole part, remainder and reduction. Two-variable guides solve each variable separately so exact fractions can be entered. Quadratic guides ask for both roots, including zero when applicable. Literal-equation guides state excluded denominators and require the requested expression; they are not a general symbolic equivalence checker.

Money is represented in integer cents. When rounding is required, prompts specify nearest cent with half cents rounded up; discount and tax are rounded at their separate stages. Tax bands are fictional training scenarios. Weighted averages use counts as weights, while round-trip speed uses total distance divided by total time. The radical family covers a linear expression under one principal square root, with positive and negative right-hand sides; the latter require rejecting the candidate after substitution.

S30 and S34 contain the same 30 exercises and keys, with different PDF headers; they share the tax/discount content rather than doubling the bank. S36 repeats the earlier literal-equation sheet but contributes its previously unused denominator-subject problem. S28 combines separately taught fraction methods into new expressions. These are source-based adaptations, not verbatim copies or complete coverage of every worksheet.

The construction diagram shows equal-radius arcs; checked inputs establish the radii, equal sides and resulting lengths. It is a diagram-based reasoning task, not a freehand compass simulator. Side-ordering problems use distinct angles; equal-angle ties are future work. The triangle range family excludes degenerate triangles and extends the worksheet with integer counting.

Inequality guides require numerical boundaries, the correct relation after division, inside/outside reasoning, and inclusion or exclusion of endpoints. The full solution appears after those checked steps. There is no interval-notation parser or interactive number-line drawing in this release. The exact quadratic-formula family uses roots of the form `center ± sqrt(radicand)` with a square-free radicand. The final typed guide step requests `center +/- sqrt(radicand)`; spacing is flexible, but it does not accept every algebraically equivalent spelling. Root counts distinguish repeated roots from two distinct solutions. General irrational quadratic-inequality boundaries and complex roots remain future material.

The generator tiers vary numerical size, signs, root types or problem structure as appropriate; not every tier is a new algebraic method. New topic groups intentionally start with introductory cases. The game is not a validated placement test or a complete entrance-exam syllabus.

The first training lock unlocks rising jumps in mission 79, spinning kicks in 80 and aerial strikes in 81. Entering a later Jade mission directly awards its prerequisite techniques after its first solved lock. Those techniques persist locally and apply within this chapter. Completing the interior safety panel stops its machinery; its second panel secures the room and restores a heart. Both panels use the same checked-step solution flow, Practice Record, scratchpad and seeded question contract as tower cases.

Fractions distinguish a fraction's value from the original numerator and denominator; reconstruction checks both before reduction. Coin and ticket models use consistent currency units and unique nonnegative counts. Interest uses I = Prt with decimal annual rates, explicitly simple interest; equal-payment questions divide the entire principal-plus-interest amount by the stated number of months, rather than modeling amortizing loans. Mixtures conserve solute and assume additive volumes. Work problems add rates or subtract a drain rate. Motion states constant ground speeds, common routes, clock origins and time units. River currents are a new application of the unequal-speed round-trip method, and upstream speed is always positive. Pythagorean problems use integer triples; trapezoids specify perpendicular heights. Probability distinguishes ordered draws, replacement and conditional second-draw counts.

Maths freezes all enemies, presses, conveyor motion, skill cooldowns and escape movement. There are no timed answers. Interior saves regenerate questions from the mission seed, validate every saved guide input in sequence, and resume the character at the safe entrance while retaining solved panels and scratch work. Final answers appear only after all guided inputs are accepted. Multiple-choice independent solving remains available until a guide is opened.

## Content contract

The pure `MathEngine` module is bounded by `BEGIN MATH ENGINE` / `END MATH ENGINE` comments in `index.html`. It does not access the DOM, network, storage or game state. `campaign` contains operation metadata and lock entries formatted as `topic:tier:templateId`.

`MathEngine.make(templateId, tier, seed)` returns:

```js
{
  id, templateId, topic, tier, seed,
  prompt, answer, answers, correct,
  hints, explanation, params,
  source: { id, pages, skill, adaptation },
  guide: [
    { prompt, answer, hint, kind, calculation? }
  ]
}
```

Each template supplies meaningful intermediate tasks. For polynomial problems these ask for coefficients and exponents; for literal equations they include symbolic expressions. A guide must have at least two checked steps. Numeric calculation metadata uses `{a, b, op}` so the tests can check each intermediate operation independently. Use `kind: 'set'` for comma-separated numerical sets (order does not matter, duplicates are rejected), and `kind: 'text'` for a short word or variable. Use `kind: 'fraction'` when equivalent rational inputs are appropriate, `number` for numerical results and `code` for a normalized exact symbolic expression. The legacy internal name `code` does not execute input: there is no `eval` or dynamic compilation.

To add a problem family:

1. Inspect the source exercise, including the rendered page when a diagram, fraction or exponent is ambiguous. Record a source ID and exact location in `SOURCES.md`.
2. Write a seeded generator with constrained, solvable parameters. Explicitly state assumptions, units, domain restrictions and rounding rules. Create distinct distractors that cannot be mathematically equivalent to the answer.
3. Write guided steps that require the learner to calculate or reason. Keep future steps and answers out of the live UI until checked. Do not put the current answer into its prompt or hint.
4. Add an independent answer oracle and any intermediate reasoning checks to `tests/math_bank.cjs`.
5. Add the template to an operation and verify the UI, particularly free input, persistence, phone scrolling, and progression.

Questions are stored in the HTML, so this is a learning interface rather than a secure examination system. Classroom text such as “circle the letter” is source material, not a command to change the app or this workflow.
